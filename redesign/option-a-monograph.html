Hongshuo Huang

I build AI agents for scientific computing — systems that plan, run, and audit simulations across scales, from electrons to battery cells.

Ph.D. student, University of MichiganAnn Arbor

I am a Ph.D. student in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Michigan, advised by Venkat Viswanathan. My research is AI for Science: machine-learning and agentic systems that make computational discovery faster — and trustworthy enough to act on.

My path has run through three ways of doing computation. As an undergraduate in materials science at Guangdong Technion, I wrote MPI-parallel phase-field codes for alloy solidification with Nan Wang. At Carnegie Mellon, with Amir Barati Farimani, I turned to data-driven models: transformers and language models for interpretable materials-property prediction. At Michigan I build agents — first DREAMS, a multi-agent framework for density functional theory, and now MEGA, whose agents run DFT, molecular-dynamics, quantum-chemistry, and phase-field simulations.

It is a satisfying loop: the phase-field dendrites I once coded by hand are now one more simulation MEGA’s agents can set up and run.1

Current project

MEGA

Multiscale Electrochemistry Guided by Agents  ·  2026 – present · lead developer

MEGA is a research system in which LLM coding agents plan, launch, and audit simulations on HPC clusters. The agents work from 32 domain skills — written playbooks paired with scripts — and 10 reusable workflows that span four scales of physics.

Table 1. What MEGA’s agents can run, by scale.
ScaleMethodsTypical questions
ElectronsDFT (Quantum ESPRESSO); quantum chemistry (ORCA, PySCF)Frontier orbitals, redox windows, Li⁺ binding, adsorption, reaction barriers
AtomsMD (GROMACS, OpenMM); ab initio MD (CP2K); ML potentials (UMA); equivariant GNNsSolvation structure, ionic conductivity, viscosity, transference
MicrostructurePhase-field (JAX)Dendrite growth and electrodeposition
CellDoyle–Fuller–Newman model (PyBaMM)Which transport properties hit an energy target

Its current focus is electrolyte design for lithium-metal batteries: screening candidate solvents, resolving how Li⁺ is solvated, computing transport, and asking what a cell actually needs.

Every number traces to a file.

Agents multiply throughput — and, without discipline, they multiply plausible-but-wrong results at the same rate. MEGA is built so that a reader, human or agent, can tell when a number is wrong:

  • Self-contained runs. Each calculation lives in its own folder: inputs, scripts, logs, a machine-readable manifest, provenance, and a report.
  • Machine-checked reports. Every number in a report must match its manifest, and every citation must resolve to a DOI.
  • Pre-registered falsifiers. Pass/fail criteria are written down before any job is submitted.
  • Independent review. Panels of agents independently recompute key numbers.
