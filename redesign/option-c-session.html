# About

I am a Ph.D. student at the University of Michigan, advised by Venkat Viswanathan. My research is AI for Science: machine-learning and agentic systems that make computational discovery faster — and trustworthy enough to act on.

My path ran through three ways of doing computation: MPI-parallel phase-field codes for alloy solidification as an undergraduate at Guangdong Technion (with Nan Wang); transformers and language models for materials-property prediction at Carnegie Mellon (with Amir Barati Farimani); and now agents at Michigan — first DREAMS for density functional theory, then MEGA.

> Full circle: the phase-field dendrites I once wrote by hand are now one more simulation MEGA’s agents can set up and run.

# Every number traces to a file.

Agents multiply throughput — and, without discipline, plausible-but-wrong results at the same rate. MEGA is built so a reader, human or agent, can tell when a number is wrong.

  • [✓]self-contained runs — inputs, scripts, logs, manifest.json, provenance.yaml, REPORT.md
  • [✓]machine-checked reports — every number must match its manifest; every citation must resolve to a DOI
  • [✓]pre-registered falsifiers — pass/fail criteria are written before any job is submitted
  • [✓]independent review — agent panels independently recompute key numbers
